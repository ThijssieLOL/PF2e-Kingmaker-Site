# Vestment Design Brainstorm

> Working document. This is not canon. It holds the settled points and the design options still open, for picking and choosing. Nothing here is written into the vault until it is chosen.

## Settled points

- **Palette A, Ash & Light.** Black stays the spine of the line at every grade. White and gold grow over it as the grades climb. The finished look is black, white, and gold together. Gold alone is rejected.
- **The line ends at Grade 2.** Grades 1 and 0 wear no vestment at all. [[The Vestments of the Clergy]] has been trimmed to match.
- **Grades 9 and 8 are finished.** They are the floor of the line and are not redrawn here.
- **Every grade is a wholly new design.** Nothing carries over as a literal object from one grade to the next, but the progression has to be obvious at a glance.
- **Nothing may hinder combat.** No trains, no floor-length capes, no loose hanging sleeves, no panels that reach the ground. Every piece above the ankle and close to the body.
- **Purely visual.** The vestments carry no magic and no armour. They look like the sun; they do not work like it. A cleric who expects combat wears armour as a separate kit.
- **Headwear climbs the line.** Grades 9 and 8 are bare-headed. From Grade 7 upward the line acquires headwear.
- **Every Zenith is blindfolded.** All Grade 3 clerics wear a blindfold, and it is a distinctive piece of the grade's look rather than a plain bandage.
- **Hand-held items are not part of the outfit.** Staffs, censers, banners, and orbs are outside the vestment.
- **Grade 2 carries a floating emblem** that never leaves the Solstice, and it is the visual indicator of the goddess's blessing.
- **Chancellery vestments are a separate pass,** later. This document covers the primary vestments by grade only.

## Design principles

### The anti-generic rule

The failure state is a high-tier cleric who reads as a generic fantasy priest: a mitre, a floor-length cloak, and gold everywhere. This line avoids all three.

- **No mitre anywhere in the line.** Headwear is a circlet, a band, or a sun-disc, never the tall split hat.
- **No floor-length cape and no train.** Capes stop at the elbow or the hip. Where a piece needs presence, it goes *up and out from the shoulders* instead of *down toward the floor*. That also keeps every grade combat-safe.
- **Gold is trim, not the whole cloth.** Gold marks edges, rays, and clasps. White carries the mass.
- **The top of the line reads as regalia, not fabric.** Rigid pieces, inlay, and worked metal appear at Grades 3 and 2 so the highest ranks stop looking like robes with more embroidery.

### The growth rule

Three things grow at once as the grades climb, so the ladder never depends on one signal:

| Signal | Grade 7 | Grade 6 | Grade 5 | Grade 4 | Grade 3 | Grade 2 |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Layer added** | Shoulder layer | Standing collar | Body panel | Outer mantle | Rigid back piece | Emblem |
| **White grows** | A band | A lining | A panel | The outer layer | The whole outer robe | Everything but the core |
| **Black retreats** | Full robe | Robe and mantle | Robe and mantle | Under-robe | Under-robe and collar | Hem, collar, cuffs |

### The reading rule

Someone who has never seen the Church should be able to line six clerics up and put them in order without being told which is which. If two neighbouring grades need a caption to tell apart, one of them is wrong.

## Grade 7 — Blaze

**Direction:** the robe stops being a uniform and starts being an office. The first thing the line adds is at the shoulders.

**Options to pick from:**

- **Shoulder cape.** A short black cape buttoned at the throat, stopping at the upper arm, gold-edged at the hem, white-lined so the white shows at the edge and flashes when he moves. The first white above a stole.
- **White front band.** The placket widens into a cream band running collar to hem, gold-worked at the edges. The priest reads as a lit strip down the middle.
- **Gold yoke.** No new layer at all. A gold-embroidered panel across the shoulders and upper chest.
- **Sleeve change.** The ties move from black ribbon to gold cord, with a white cuff showing below the tie.
- **Two-band collar.** The collar gains a second gold band, thin and set a finger's width below the first.

**Headwear:** a thin gold band at the brow, no ornament.

**Combat note:** the cape is short and buttons flat to the chest. The front band is a flat panel, not a hanging stole.

---

## Grade 6 — Ignis

**Direction:** authority becomes visible from behind. The first collar that stands up, and the first time white is a lining rather than a stripe.

**Options to pick from:**

- **High fan collar.** A stiff collar rising behind the neck in a shallow fan, black outside and white inside, with gold rays stitched on the inner face so they show when the light catches it. Reads as a fan of light behind the head without being a halo.
- **Elbow mantle.** A black mantle to the elbow, white-lined, gold-edged. The white flashes with every movement.
- **Widened stole.** The Grade 8 stole grows into a full band crossing both shoulders and hanging to the knee, its gold vine giving way to gold rays.
- **Rayed shoulders.** Gold rays spread from the collarbones outward along the shoulder line, short and straight.
- **Bishop's band.** A wide cream band at the cuff and hem, so the lower edge of the robe is white.

**Headwear:** the gold band with a single ray rising at the front.

**Combat note:** the fan collar is stiff and short. The mantle buttons to the chest and does not flap.

---

## Grade 5 — Pyre

**Direction:** the first real silhouette change, and the first grade with a body panel. Something hangs at the front, and it is white.

**Options to pick from:**

- **White tabard.** A white panel from the chest to the knee, worn over the black robe, gold-bordered, with a gold sun worked at the chest. The clearest "this one outranks the last one" signal in the whole line.
- **Solar breastpiece.** A flat gold-worked plate over the sternum on a white band, rayed outward. Regalia, not armour, and it does not stop a blade.
- **White-lined mantle.** The mantle turns white at the hem and inner face, so the archbishop's outline is white from any angle.
- **Rayed back.** Gold rays spread from the shoulders down the back of the mantle, widening as they fall.
- **Nine-fold cuff.** Nine small gold flame points worked along each cuff, one per chancellery.

**Headwear:** a circlet of nine small flame points.

**Combat note:** the tabard splits at the hips or buttons to the sides so it cannot trip him.

---

## Grade 4 — Vesta

**Direction:** white turns outward. Black survives as the inner layer and the under-robe. This is the pivot of the whole palette.

**Options to pick from:**

- **White mantle, black robe.** The mantle turns white outside over the black robe, gold-edged at every seam. Simple and strong.
- **Layered panels.** Black robe, white mantle, gold corona at the chest. Three readable layers, the first grade that reads as a *set* rather than a garment.
- **White over-robe.** A long white over-robe open at the front, gold at every edge, black showing in the opening. The white moves as a single mass.
- **Rayed back-collar, first form.** A stiff black collar rising behind the shoulders with gold rays fanning out from it, white inside. The first rigid piece in the line.
- **Asymmetric split.** One shoulder white, one black, divided down the centre. Theatrical, but it weakens the sense of one order. Included only so it can be rejected.

**Headwear:** a taller gold circlet with a small sun-disc at the front.

**Combat note:** the over-robe opens at the front and has no closed train. The back-collar is rigid and rides above armour.

---

## Grade 3 — Zenith

**Direction:** architecture. The line stops describing cloth and starts describing structure. Everything is white and gold over a black core, and the first piece that is plainly metal appears. The blindfold is the grade's defining mark, and it is the first thing anyone notices.

**Options to pick from:**

- **Rayed back-collar.** A stiff gold-rayed collar rising behind the shoulders, black outside and white inside, the rays fanning wider than the shoulders. Presence without a cape, and nothing trails.
- **Corona mantle.** A white mantle with a full gold corona worked across the back, rays radiating from a central disc.
- **All white, gold seams.** White robe and white mantle over a black under-robe, gold at every edge. The simplest and most radiant read.
- **Gold shoulder rays.** Rigid gold rays rising from each shoulder like a yoke, standing off the body by a hand's width. Closer to regalia than clothing.
- **Sunstone accents.** Small sunstone settings at the collar and cuffs, the first appearance of the stone anywhere in the line.

### The blindfold

Settled: every Zenith wears one, and it is part of the grade's design. What it looks like is open.

- **Black band, gold rays.** A wide black band across the eyes with gold rays radiating from the centre, white cloth behind. The one black element on an otherwise white and gold figure, so the eyes become the darkest thing on him.
- **Gold sun-disc.** A single gold disc over both eyes, worked as a sun. He wears the sun where his eyes were.
- **Plain white band.** No ornament at all, gold-edged only. The most absolute version, because it gives nothing back to whoever looks at it.
- **Layered veils.** Several thin bands of white cloth wound over the eyes, gold thread worked between the layers so the gold shows in the seams.
- **Gold filigree over black.** A black band cased in open gold filigree shaped as rays, so the gold reads as a cage over the darkness.
- **Closed-eye device.** A gold-worked closed eye at the centre of the band, the one motif that says the eyes behind it are shut on purpose.

**Headwear:** the rayed circlet sits over the blindfold, so the two read as one piece. Alternative: the blindfold replaces the circlet entirely and the sun-disc is worked into the blindfold itself.

**Combat note:** the back-collar is rigid, short, and rides above armour. Shoulder rays are fixed, not loose.

---

## Grade 2 — Solstice

**Direction:** black survives only as the core. The hem, the collar, and the cuffs are the last places it shows. Everything else is white and gold, and the emblem does the rest.

**Options to pick from:**

- **White and gold over a black core.** White outer robe, gold at every edge, black under-robe visible at hem, collar, and cuffs. The light clothes a dark centre, which is the whole idea of palette A in one silhouette.
- **All white, black at the throat.** White robe and mantle, gold seams, black only at the collar. Cleaner and colder, but it loses the spine.
- **Layered regalia.** Black under-robe, white robe, white mantle, gold corona across the back, sunstone at the chest. The fullest version of the line.
- **Gold-edged panels.** Flat white panels layered front and back, each bordered in gold, cut above the knee so nothing drags.

**Reserved for Grade 2 only (pick any or all):**

- **Sunstone.** No grade below 2 may wear it. It is the clearest single "this is the Solstice" marker, and it reads at a distance.
- **The nine-flame motif.** Nine flames in one device, one per chancellery, worn only by the one who commands all nine.
- **Pure white.** Every other grade uses cream. Grade 2 is the only grade whose cloth is white.
- **The rayed gold back-collar,** if it is not already used at Grade 3.

**Headwear:** bare. The emblem is the crown.

**Combat note:** the panels are cut above the knee and the mantle stops at the elbow.

---

## The Solstice emblem

**Shape, taken from the Church's own sun logo:** an open golden arc, roughly three-quarters of a circle, with a tapered point curling off one end, and detached flame-shaped rays radiating from the other side. Rendered as a hollow ring of metal with nothing at its centre. The empty centre is Grade 0's seat, the goddess's place, which no mortal may stand in. The Solstice stands inside the sun's circle and never at its middle.

**Scale and placement:** about a pace across, floating a hand's breadth behind the shoulders at head height. Turned so it reads as a thin bright line in profile and a full circle from the front.

**Always present.** It never leaves him. It is the visual indicator of the goddess's blessing, so its absence has to mean something.

**Idle animation options:**

- **Drifting rays.** The nine flame rays orbit the arc slowly at different speeds, so the corona never sits still.
- **Slow turn.** The whole emblem rotates almost imperceptibly, one revolution every minute or so.
- **Flicker pulse.** Each flame brightens and dims on its own rhythm, like a candle in still air.
- **Breathing arc.** The arc brightens along its length in a slow wave, once every few seconds.
- **Combination.** Drifting rays plus the breathing arc. The rays give it life, the arc gives it a heartbeat.

**Absence and visibility options:**

- **What the absence looks like:** the emblem goes dark, or the arc closes into a full circle, or the rays droop and fade, or it vanishes entirely. The closed circle is the most ominous, since it is the shape the goddess never wears.
- **Who sees it:** everyone sees it, or only the faithful see the rays while outsiders see dull metal, or it is plainly visible but only the Church knows what it means.
- **Does it react:** it could brighten in the presence of undead, dim during the Solstice's own doubt, or hold perfectly still in a temple.

## Line-wide choices

### Signature element

One element that appears at Grade 9 and climbs all the way to 2, so the line reads as one order. Pick one, or one primary and one secondary.

- **The sun buckle.** Already canon at Grade 8 as a brass buckle stamped with a sun. It climbs: belt buckle, then chest clasp, then the centrepiece of the Grade 2 chest.
- **The black core.** The under-robe stays black at every grade, shrinking until only the hem, collar, and cuffs show. It is the spine of palette A and costs nothing to keep.
- **The cream band.** A white band down the front, widening as the grades climb until it becomes the Grade 2 outer robe.
- **The sleeve tie.** Black ribbon at 9, gold cord at 7, white ribbon at 5, bare gold cuff at 3, nothing at 2.
- **The high collar.** Present from Grade 9, growing taller and stiffer until it becomes the Grade 3 rayed back-collar.

### Craft and origin

- **A dedicated atelier.** A sanctified workshop, perhaps under [[The Chancellery of Prismatic Inquiry]], which already handles solar-prism work and divine energy. Explains sunstone, gold work, and consistency across the line.
- **Made as penance.** The cleric works their own vestment as part of the ascension rite, and the Church's tailors finish the gold. Fits [[Tattoos of Penance]] and the redeemed-sinner theology.
- **Inherited.** Each vestment passes down from the previous holder of the grade, repaired and refitted. Gives every garment a history.
- **Commissioned at each ascension.** New vestment at every grade, made to the new rank, and the old one is surrendered.

### Motif ladder

- **Proposed unlock order:** gold vine and floral scrollwork at 8 (already canon), rays at 7, the corona at 5, the rayed disc at 3, the nine flames at 2.
- **Alternative:** the sun disc appears early and gains detail as it climbs, ending as a fully rayed disc at 2.
- **Open question worth flagging:** whether the sun is ever given a face. A face in the sun could read as depicting the goddess, which may be heresy, or as depicting the Solstice, which may be worse. Left unresolved on purpose.

### Grade 2 status

- **One-of-a-kind.** Made for the current Solstice, never repeated. The emblem's inner rim carries his name.
- **Fixed design.** Every Solstice wears the same vestment, and only the emblem is personal. Makes the office larger than the man.

### The ending of the document

- **Hard ceiling.** The vestments stop at Grade 2 because Grade 2 is the highest a mortal may reach. No hook, no implication of more.
- **Soft hook.** A closing line noting that Grade 1 is a change beyond clothing, which the Church does not describe.

### Combat rule

- **One rule for all.** Above the ankle, sleeves tied, no train, no closed cape, at every grade.
- **Two sets at the top.** Grades 3 and 2 get a full ceremonial set and a plainer field set, since a Solstice may lead a war and still hold a high rite.

### Entry length

- **One paragraph per grade,** matching Spark and Cinder.
- **One paragraph to Grade 5, longer above,** so the top three grades get the room to describe the added layers and the emblem.

## Open questions

### The Zenith blindfold

1. **Why are the Zeniths blindfolded?** A vow, a rite at ascension, a punishment, a price paid for what they were allowed to see, or something else entirely.
2. **Are they actually blind, or does the blindfold cover working eyes?** If they are blind, what lets a Grade 3 cleric still function at that level?
3. **Worn always, or ceremony only?** In combat too, or does it come off for war?
4. **Does the Solstice wear one as well,** or does the blindfold stop at Grade 3?
5. **Do the laity know what is behind it?** Public knowledge, or something the Church keeps quiet?
6. **What does the blindfold look like** from the options above, and does the circlet sit over it or does it replace the circlet?
7. **Is the blindfold a vestment at all,** or a personal item each Zenith has made for himself?

### Everything else

1. Does the emblem go dark or vanish if the goddess withdraws her blessing, and what does that look like?
2. Do the unfaithful see the emblem, or does it read as plain metal to anyone outside the faith?
3. Is the Grade 2 vestment one-of-a-kind or a fixed design?
4. Which signature element carries the whole line?
5. Who makes the vestments, and are they made new or inherited?
6. Does the document end as a ceiling or leave a hook toward Grade 1?
7. Do the top grades get a ceremonial set and a field set, or one rule for all?
8. How long is each grade's entry?
9. Which grade options above are keepers, and which go in the rejected pile?
