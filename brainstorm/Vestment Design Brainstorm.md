# Vestment Design Brainstorm

> Working document. This is not canon. It records the settled design for the primary vestments by grade, the principles that shaped it, and the art prompts built from it.

## Settled points

- **Palette A, Ash & Light.** Black stays the spine of the line at every grade. White and gold grow over it as the grades climb. The finished look is black, white, and gold together. Gold alone was rejected.
- **The line ends at Grade 2.** Grades 1 and 0 wear no vestment at all. [[The Vestments of the Clergy]] has been trimmed to match.
- **Grades 9 and 8 are finished.** They are the floor of the line, and their art exists.
- **Every grade is a wholly new design.** Nothing carries over as a literal object from one grade to the next, but the progression has to be obvious at a glance.
- **Every grade looks stronger than the grade below it.** Not richer and not busier. Stronger.
- **Every figure stays tall and narrow.** Bulk reads as soft, and this line is meant to read as powerful.
- **Nothing may hinder combat.** No trains, no floor-length capes, no loose hanging sleeves, no panels that reach the ground.
- **Purely visual.** The vestments carry no magic and no armour. A cleric who expects combat wears armour as a separate kit.
- **No headwear anywhere in the line.** No crown, circlet, band, mitre, or sun-disc on the head at any grade.
- **Every Zenith is blindfolded.** All Grade 3 clerics wear one. It sits on the face rather than the crown of the head.
- **Gold flames are worked on both the black and the white cloth** at every grade from 7 up.
- **Hand-held items are not part of the outfit.** Staffs, censers, banners, and orbs sit outside the vestment.
- **Grade 2 carries a huge floating sun emblem** behind his back, wider than his shoulders, and it is the visual indicator of the goddess's blessing.
- **Chancellery vestments are a separate pass,** later. This document covers the primary vestments by grade only.

## Design principles

### The anti-generic rule

The failure state is a high-tier cleric who reads as a generic fantasy priest: a mitre, a floor-length cloak, and gold everywhere. This line avoids all three.

- **No headwear, no hood, ever.** A hood is the fastest way back to generic priest.
- **Capes are welcome, generic cloaks are not.** A cape gives a high grade its presence. What is out is the hooded, floor-length, round-clasped, billowing cloak.
- **Gold is trim, not the whole cloth.** Gold marks edges, rays, and clasps. White carries the mass.
- **The top of the line reads as regalia, not fabric.** Rigid pieces, inlay, and worked metal arrive at Grades 3 and 2 so the highest ranks stop looking like robes with more embroidery.

### The silhouette rule

Every figure stays tall and narrow, and the shoulders are the widest point of the body. Extra layers add vertical lines, not width.

- **The mantle is widest across the shoulders, never at the hem.** It hangs in straight vertical folds from the shoulder medallions instead of flaring into a bell.
- **Each layer is narrower than the layer above it,** so the skirt tapers toward the hem rather than ballooning out.
- **A bulky figure is a failed design.** Volume reads as soft and heavy, which is the opposite of the power the line is chasing.

### The power rule

Every grade has to look stronger than the grade below it. Someone who has never seen the Church should be able to line the clerics up and put them in order without being told which is which. If two neighbouring grades need a caption to tell them apart, one of them is wrong.

The power comes from the aesthetic, not from armour. The vestments carry none and the shoulder pieces are regalia, so a high grade reads as dangerous and elevated while wearing cloth and worked metal. Five levers do that work:

- **Structure.** Soft cloth reads as humility. Rigid pieces, stiffened collars, and built garments read as authority.
- **Height.** A collar that rises, a rayed back piece that climbs above the shoulders. With no headwear in the line, everything that reads as rank grows upward from the shoulders.
- **Material.** Wool, then heavy linen, then silk, then cloth of gold, then worked metal, then sunstone. Metal outranks cloth every time.
- **Radiance.** Matte at the bottom, sheen in the middle, polish and gleam at the top. The lower grades absorb light, the higher grades catch it.
- **Retreat of black.** Black is the humble colour. As it shrinks from the full robe down to the collar, cuffs, and hem, the figure grows more radiant.

### The power ladder

One dominant signal per grade, each strictly stronger than the one below. A grade may not borrow the signal of the grade above it.

| Grade | Dominant signal | Why it reads stronger |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **9** | Plainness | Deliberate austerity. Nothing has been earned yet. |
| **8** | The first ornament | Gold buttons and a stole. The first grade that is decorated at all. |
| **7** | The first added layer | The silhouette gains shoulders. |
| **6** | The first rigid piece | The collar stands up behind the neck. Structure arrives. |
| **5** | The first metal | A worked plate and a belt plate. Metal outranks cloth. |
| **4** | The inversion | White turns outward. The figure becomes the light instead of wearing it. |
| **3** | Architecture and the mark | A rigid rayed collar wider than the shoulders, and the face is taken away. |
| **2** | The emblem | A sun floating at his back, and the only sunstone in the line. |

**Guard rails:** metal does not appear before Grade 5, rigidity not before Grade 6, white does not become the outer layer before Grade 4, and sunstone appears at Grade 2 only. If a lower grade picks up a higher grade's signal by accident, the higher grade needs a new one.

### The growth rule

Three things grow at once, so the ladder never depends on a single signal.

| Signal | Grade 7 | Grade 6 | Grade 5 | Grade 4 | Grade 3 | Grade 2 |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Layer added** | Shoulder layer | Standing collar | Body panel | Outer mantle | Rigid back piece | Emblem |
| **White grows** | A band | A lining | A panel | The outer layer | The whole outer robe | Everything but the core |
| **Black retreats** | Full robe | Robe and mantle | Robe and mantle | Under-robe | Under-robe and collar | Hem, collar, cuffs |

### Layering and extravagance

The first lineup came back too plain at the top: the highest rank read as a single cone of cloth with a hem pattern while the middle ranks looked richer. The cause was the wording. A prompt that names one robe gets one robe drawn, no matter how much embroidery is listed on it. So the layers are stated outright, and the count climbs with the grade.

| Grade | Distinct layers | What is visible |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **7** | 2 | Robe, capelet |
| **6** | 3 | Robe, mantle, fan collar |
| **5** | 4 | Robe, stole panel, mantle, shoulder discs and chain |
| **4** | 5 | Under-robe, robe, mantle, chain, bracer |
| **3** | 6 | Under-robe, robe, mantle, rayed back collar, asymmetric shoulder ornament, blindfold |
| **2** | 7 | Under-robe, robe, over-robe, mantle, hanging panels, shoulder sunbursts, chest band and chain |

**Rules that make a figure read as extravagant without making it bulky:**

- **Break the outline.** From Grade 5 up, something sticks out past the body: shoulder discs, rays, a fan collar, hanging panels. A smooth A-line silhouette reads as simple no matter how much embroidery sits on it.
- **Stack the hems.** Two or three hems at different heights in different colours, each narrower than the one above, so the bottom reads as layers instead of one skirt.
- **Layer the sleeves.** From Grade 4 up, a wide outer sleeve over a fitted inner sleeve, with a gold cuff on each.
- **Add one more piece of worked metal per grade.** Discs, chains, bracers, collars, shoulder pieces. The top grade carries the most metal in the line.
- **Extravagance lives in the detail, not in the volume.** The top grade wins on piece count and worked metal, never on width.

### The gold flame motif

Gold flame tongues are worked across both the black robe and the white mantle, and they are the best unifying motif the line has. Fire is the Church's primary element, and every grade is already named after a flame.

**The rule:** gold flames appear on **both** the black and the white cloth from Grade 7 up. Gold on black reads as fire in the dark, gold on cream reads as fire in daylight. Neither cloth is left plain.

| Grade | Flame work |
| :---- | :---- |
| **9** | None. The Spark is plain, and the plainness is the point. |
| **8** | Gold vine and floral scrollwork on the cream stole (canon). No flames yet. |
| **7** | The first flame tongues, small and few, at the cuffs and the hem of the capelet. |
| **6** | Flames rise along the stole, replacing the vine, and along the hem of the mantle. |
| **5** | Flames climb the front edges of the mantle and the stole panel, and the skirt of the black robe is worked with them. |
| **4** | Flames fill the lower half of the mantle, rising from the hem, and run along the black under-robe. |
| **3** | Flames cover the mantle from hem to shoulder, and the black robe is worked to match. |
| **2** | Flames over both garments edge to edge, with the nine-flame device at the chest. |

**They always point upward,** from the hem toward the shoulders, so the figure reads as a flame itself. At the top grades the whole garment is a fire climbing the body.

## The designs, grade by grade

Each entry gives the grade's signal, its layer count, and the garment itself. The garment text doubles as the prompt line under the base prompt.

### Grade 7 — Blaze

**Signal:** the first added layer. **Layers:** 2.

A black ankle-length robe with gold knotted buttons and a gold-trimmed high collar, with small gold flame tongues rising from the hem, and a cream stole crossing both shoulders worked in gold vine with a few small gold flames. Over it, a short black shoulder capelet resting on the shoulders and stopping at the upper arm, its hem edged in gold and lined in cream, with small gold flames rising from the hem. A cream band down the front placket, gold-worked at the edges, gold cord sleeve ties, and a brown leather belt with a brass sun buckle.

*Combat note:* the capelet is short and buttons flat to the chest. The front band is a flat panel, not a hanging stole.

### Grade 6 — Ignis

**Signal:** the first rigid piece. **Layers:** 3.

A black ankle-length robe with gold flame tongues rising along its hem, under a black elbow-length mantle lined in cream and edged in gold, its hem falling straight and no wider than the robe beneath it. A stiff fan-shaped collar rises behind the neck, black outside and white inside, with fine gold rays stitched on the inner face. The cream stole has widened into a full band crossing both shoulders and falling to the knee, worked in rising gold flames. A gold chain crosses the chest.

*Combat note:* the fan collar is stiff and short. The mantle buttons to the chest and does not flap.

### Grade 5 — Pyre

**Signal:** the first metal. **Layers:** 4.

A black ankle-length robe with gold flames along the skirt, a broad cream stole panel embroidered with a sunburst and rising gold flames and finished with gold fringe, and over both a cape that rests on the shoulders, black outside and cream inside, gold-edged, falling open at the front with gold flame tongues climbing the front edges. The cape is widest across the shoulders and hangs in straight vertical folds, its hem above the ankle and no wider than the robe beneath it. Two plain gold discs sit on the shoulders, joined by a plain gold chain across the chest, and a brown leather belt with a large circular gold sun buckle closes the robe.

*Combat note:* the mantle rests on the shoulders with the front open, so the arms stay free.

### Grade 4 — Vesta

**Signal:** the inversion. **Layers:** 5.

A black under-robe and a black ankle-length robe worked with gold flames, under a shoulder mantle that turns white outside with a black lining, gold-edged, its lower half filled with gold flame tongues rising from the hem, with a small rayed gold sun disc on each front edge and wide layered sleeves over fitted inner sleeves. The two shoulder discs are rayed gold, joined by a gold chain carrying small sun discs. A gold bracer on one forearm, and gold ray and star embroidery at the ankles. Every layer below the mantle is narrower than the layer above it, so the skirt tapers to the hem.

*Combat note:* the mantle opens at the front and has no closed train.

### Grade 3 — Zenith

**Signal:** architecture and the mark. **Layers:** 6.

A black under-robe and a black high-collared robe, under a white mantle resting on the shoulders, white outside with a black lining, covered from hem to shoulder in rising gold flame tongues, with wide layered sleeves. On the left shoulder only, a large asymmetric ornament of long gold rays and blades fans outward and downward, with no sun disc at its centre, and a gold sunburst disc wider than the shoulder sits on the right shoulder. Between the two shoulders rises a stiff gold-rayed back collar, its rays fanning wider than the body. The eyes are covered by a wide black blindfold banded with gold rays radiating from the centre. A gold rayed band crosses the chest, and sunstone accents sit at the collar and cuffs. The mantle hangs straight and narrow, so the shoulders stay the widest point of the figure.

*Combat note:* the back-collar is rigid, short, and rides above armour. Shoulder rays are fixed, not loose.

### Grade 2 — Solstice

**Signal:** the emblem. **Layers:** 7, the most in the line.

A black under-robe showing at the hem, the collar, and the cuffs. Over it a white ankle-length robe with a gold-edged hem. Over that a white over-robe with wide layered sleeves, each finished with a gold flame cuff. Over that the broadest mantle in the order, white outside with a black lining, gold-edged, its hem cut into ray points, resting on the shoulders with the arms free and the hem above the ankle, covered edge to edge in gold flame tongues rising from the hem. The mantle is widest across the shoulders and falls in straight vertical folds, no wider at the hem than at the chest, and every layer beneath it is narrower than the one above, so the figure stays tall and narrow. Two large gold sunbursts sit on the shoulders with sunstone at their centres, joined by a gold rayed band and a chain across the chest. Gold-edged hanging panels at the front carry the nine-flame device and a sunstone. The head is bare, and behind him floats a huge hollow gold sun emblem, far wider than his shoulders, so it reads clearly past both sides of his body at the height of his back rather than his neck: an open arc of gold roughly three-quarters of a circle with a tapered point curling off one end, and nine detached flame-shaped rays radiating from it, with nothing at the centre.

**Reserved for Grade 2 only:** sunstone, the nine-flame device, and pure white cloth, where every grade below uses cream.

*Combat note:* the panels are cut above the knee and the mantle stops at the elbow.

## The shoulder-set mantle

The mantle is the line's signature cape form, and it arrived from a reference the user liked: a very broad cape that **rests on the shoulders** instead of hanging from a clasp at the throat. It runs from Grade 5 up.

![[Vestment concept - broad shoulder mantle.png]]

- **It rests on the shoulders.** No clasp at the throat and no chain holding it shut at the neck. The weight sits on the shoulder line, anchored at the shoulder medallions, which keeps the head clear for the collar and the blindfold.
- **It is widest across the shoulders, then falls straight.** No bell shape and no flare at the hem, so it adds sweep without adding bulk.
- **Black outside, cream inside** at Grade 5. From Grade 4 it inverts to white outside with a black lining, which carries that grade's inversion signal on the shoulders where it reads best.
- **Gold at the hem and the front edges only.** The cloth carries no all-over gold.
- **The front stays open,** so the arms are free and it never restricts the hands.
- **Hem above the ankle.** It hangs, it does not trail.

**Shoulder sun medallions.** Two gold sun discs sit on the shoulders where the mantle rests, and a smaller disc is set on each front edge. Plain discs at Grade 5, rayed at Grade 4, full sunbursts wider than the shoulder at Grade 3, and sunstone centres at Grade 2. They are regalia, not armour, and they stop nothing.

**The chest chain.** A gold chain runs between the two shoulder medallions. It adds gold presence without gold cloth, and it explains how the mantle sits: the chain ties the two anchors together. Plain at Grade 5, carrying small sun discs at Grade 4, a rayed band at Grade 3, and set with sunstone at Grade 2.

## The asymmetric rayed shoulder

The one piece in the line that breaks symmetry, and the reason it feels unique.

![[Vestment concept - asymmetric rayed shoulder.png]]

- **Left shoulder only,** so it never reads as a pair of pauldrons.
- **Long gold rays and blades of uneven length,** fanning outward and downward from a low gold base that follows the shoulder line.
- **No sun disc at the centre.** The rays are the whole ornament. A disc would turn it into a sunburst, and sunbursts already belong to the shoulder medallions.
- **Worn at Grade 3,** where it joins the rayed collar and the blindfold as the Zenith's three marks.
- **Regalia, not armour.** It stops nothing.

## The Zenith blindfold

Every Grade 3 cleric wears one, and it is the grade's defining mark.

**The design:** a wide black band across the eyes with gold rays radiating from its centre, white cloth behind. It is the one black element on an otherwise white and gold figure, so the eyes become the darkest thing on him.

## The Solstice emblem

**Shape, taken from the Church's own sun logo:** an open golden arc, roughly three-quarters of a circle, with a tapered point curling off one end, and detached flame-shaped rays radiating from the other side. Rendered as a hollow ring of metal with nothing at its centre. The empty centre is Grade 0's seat, the goddess's place, which no mortal may stand in, so the Solstice stands inside the sun's circle and never at its middle.

**Scale and placement:** huge. The emblem is far wider than his shoulders, so the arc and the rays read clearly past both sides of his body, and it sits behind his **back** at chest and waist height rather than behind his neck. The top of the arc rises above his shoulder line and the bottom falls below his belt, so in a front view the ring frames the whole torso instead of the head.

**Always present.** It never leaves him, because it is the visual indicator of the goddess's blessing, and its absence has to mean something.

## Still open

1. **Cream or white at the top.** The base prompt fixes the palette at cream, while Grades 4 to 2 are written as white. Either cream holds all the way up and only Grade 2 is true white, or the palette line changes for the top grades.
2. **Who makes the vestments,** and whether they are made new at each ascension or inherited from the previous holder.
3. **Whether the Grade 2 vestment is one-of-a-kind** for the current Solstice or a fixed design every Solstice inherits.
4. **How the document ends,** as a hard ceiling at Grade 2 or with a hook toward Grade 1.
5. **Whether the top grades get a ceremonial set and a field set,** or one rule for all.
6. **Why the Zeniths are blindfolded,** and whether the blindness is literal.
