## What it touches

The hub page carries the tension in two sentences. The Church "runs its affairs with the scale and discipline of a sovereign empire," and its nine High Chancelleries each "govern a distinct pillar of statecraft, divine law, and civil service." Yet the same page says it "does not seek to rule nations directly" and holds "a strict apolitical stance on state governance." Both readings are canon, so the question is where one ends and the other begins.

## Where the Church stops short of a state

Four things separate it from a realm, and they are the hinge of the whole question:

- **It rules no land.** Clergy are forbidden crowns, secular rule, and political office, so the Church holds no territory and governs no population as subjects.
- **It owns no means of production,** and it leaves banking to the state or to honest private owners.
- **It intervenes rather than occupies.** Its reach into a state runs from public denouncement and the excommunication of rulers to the covert commando strike, not to annexation.
- **Its head is not chosen by the world.** No inheritance, election, or conquest picks the Solstice; the goddess names them when the seat empties.

## The distinction, as it stands

A state governs a territory and everyone on it, by law backed by force. The Church governs its own members and its own houses, by doctrine, and reaches beyond them only where doctrine says a ruler has crossed a line. On paper that is a clean split.

In practice, the Church runs schools, hospitals, courts, an army, an intelligence arm, a treasury, a registry, and a courier service. That is a state's apparatus with the land left out. The open question this document exists to answer is whether the Church is borrowing a state's tools to serve a mission, or whether it is a state that has chosen not to annex.

## Questions

**Territory and membership**

1. Does the Church hold territory of its own, or does it exist wholly inside other realms?
2. Who owns a church's buildings and land: the Church, the local state, or the donors who gave them?
3. Is membership a legal status like citizenship, or purely religious? Can a person be a solite and still not obey church law?
4. Do the faithful answer to church courts wherever they live, or only where a state grants the Church jurisdiction?
5. When church law and the law of the land disagree over the same person, which one holds?
6. Are the courts open to non-members, or do they try clergy and solites only?

**Money and services**

7. Is the tithe voluntary giving, a tax the Church levies, or a fee for rites? Who enforces non-payment?
8. The Church runs schools, hospitals, orphanages, and relief. Does it act as the state's contractor, a rival provider, or does it claim these as its own right?
9. When the Church feeds the poor in a land whose ruler will not pay for it, whose subjects does it make them?
10. The Ember Post crosses any distance. Is it a mail service for members, or a utility that states come to depend on?
11. The Meridian Cadastre keeps registries and archives of the people it serves. Is that a civil registry in function, and who may read it?

**Force and intelligence**

12. The Church keeps an army and has fought holy wars. Under whose flag does it march: its own, or a host state's?
13. The Silent Illumination runs espionage and internal oversight. Is that a state's intelligence service by another name, and how does it square with the apolitical doctrine?
14. Where a state bans the faith, what is the Church there: outlawed, an underground church, or an unrecognised mission?

**The apolitical stance and the veto**

15. The ban on crowns, rule, and office: is it doctrine, law, treaty, or all three?
16. Who enforces the ban on the Church's own clergy, and what is the penalty for a cleric who takes a crown?
17. Does the ban bind only clergy, or lay solites who hold office as well?
18. What exactly does the Moral Veto authorise once issued, and must a ruler obey it? The Purifying Flame page already sets who may issue one (any Grade 7 and up may start it, and it needs a group of Grade 6 or one Grade 5); what it binds is still open.
19. Is the Moral Veto a legal power that states have agreed to, or a moral claim the Church enforces by its own means?
20. Can the Church be wrong about a veto? Is there any appeal, and who would hear it?

**How the world sees it**

21. Do secular rulers see the Church as a rival sovereign, a partner, or a threat?
22. Does the Church want believers or subjects? Could it become a state, or would that break its own doctrine?
23. Where does [[Aurelius]]'s fledgling kingdom in the Stolen Lands sit against all this: a banned realm, a host, or a future partner?

**Left over**

24. Is there any state-like apparatus the table above has missed, something the Church does that no note records yet?
