### A. The doctrine itself (the theory)

1. Is the economics a formal doctrine with an in-world name (a creed, a "Mandate") — or is "the Shared Hearth" itself the term used for the whole system?
2. At its core, is property privately held with surplus redistributed, or does the Church own the major means of production directly?
3. Canon says "high income is not a sin, but hoarding is." Where exactly is that line drawn — a proportional cap, a test of intent, a communal judgment? And who decides a person has crossed it?
4. Does the Church charge interest, or condemn usury? Is lending at profit banned, capped, or allowed?
5. Do the clergy take a personal vow of poverty, or only the institution practice anti-hoarding? Does a Grade 5 own their own things?
6. Is giving framed as atonement/penance, as civic duty, or both? Do the [[Tattoos of Penance]] connect to paying for sin?
7. What is the Church's teaching on inheritance and noble wealth? (This is what the banned kingdoms react to — I'd like the specific doctrine.)
8. Is there an in-world creed that reads like "from each by ability, to each by need," and if so, what is its actual wording?
9. How does the doctrine treat the very rich who give generously versus the comfortably-off who give nothing? Is there a category of "acceptable wealth"?

### B. Where the money comes from

10. Tithes: a fixed percentage of income, a flat sum, or voluntary? Expected of all believers or only clergy/members?
11. Is tithing enforced (with consequences) or invited (with honour)? Who may refuse?
12. Beyond tithes: what else funds the Church? (land rents, investments, sale of goods/relics, fees for rites, banking, church-owned businesses, state subsidies)
13. Does the Church own land, mines, or guild shares, and collect rent from them?
14. Are there fees for services — cremation, weddings, healing, schooling? Are the poor always exempt, and who proves poverty?
15. Does the Church act as a bank or lender? Can it hold deposits, issue loans, or move value as letters of credit (tied to [[The Ember Post]])?
16. How much income is coin versus in-kind (grain, labour, goods)?
17. When you say "**pledges**" — do you mean a pledged share of income, a pledge of service/labour, a vow of giving, or a financial instrument? Please define it, since this is the term driving the whole idea.
18. Tax relationship with secular states: do governments exempt, subsidise, or seize the Church? In banned kingdoms, how does funding operate covertly?
19. How are tithes physically collected from village to parish to diocese to headquarters, and what stops a local collector from skimming?

### C. Where the money goes

20. What is the rough budget split — clergy pay, relief, buildings, military, intelligence, schools, missions? Is the split fixed doctrine or set annually?
21. How does pay scale across the [[The Grade System|grades]]? Does it come with board and lodging, or is it coin only?
22. Is the Shared Hearth a **net payer** that funds the other eight chancelleries (including [[The Chancellery of Silent Illumination|Silent Illumination]] and [[The Chancellery of Celestial Aegis|Celestial Aegis]]), or does each chancellery raise its own funds?
23. Who sets budgets — Shared Hearth for everyone, or each chancellery petitioning for its share?
24. Given the anti-hoarding doctrine, how large a reserve is the Church allowed to keep? Is there a cap on the treasury itself?
25. What happens first in a famine or shortfall — who eats, who gets cut?
26. Is the Church allowed to invest for profit, or does investment count as hoarding?

### D. The pledge system

27. Who makes a pledge — lay believers, nobles, whole towns, institutions?
28. Is a pledge binding in law, in honour, or by **magic** (oath, geas, a binding rite)? A magically enforced pledge would be a very strong lever.
29. What is the consequence of a broken pledge — penance, debt, excommunication, seizure of property?
30. Is a pledge recorded in a ledger, and by whom — Shared Hearth, or [[The Chancellery of the Meridian Cadastre|the Meridian Cadastre]]?
31. Can pledges be inherited or transferred (a family's pledge passing down generations)?
32. Do pledges substitute for taxes or labour in lands the Church effectively administers?

### E. The charity organizations and how they're organised

33. What are the distinct charity types (feeding, housing, orphanages, medical, disaster, burial, work-relief), and is there a formal taxonomy?
34. How are they structured *logically* — one body per population tier, a branch at every church, or split across chancelleries? Which chancellery owns which category (health with [[The Chancellery of Consecrated Solace|Consecrated Solace]], schooling with [[The Chancellery of Sacred Canon|Sacred Canon]], etc.)?
35. How does a needy person actually access relief — referral from a parish, means-testing, open door? Is receiving aid dignified and private, or public and stigmatised?
36. Is relief conditional (work for food, attend mass, take a pledge)? Could it be gamed?
37. When the Church partners with a secular state on welfare, who pays, who runs it, and who takes the credit?
38. Orphanages feed directly into chancellery recruitment. Is the tension acknowledged in-world that charity is also a supply of recruits and labour?
39. Does the Church charge rent on its public housing, or provide it free? Who is eligible?
40. Are any groups excluded from relief (unbelievers, heretics, criminals, [[The Grand Order of the Phoenix]])?

### F. The Chancellery of the Shared Hearth itself

41. What are its internal divisions (Treasury, Tithes, Relief, Audit, Investment...), and do they have names?
42. Who leads it — what grade — and what are its internal offices beneath the leader?
43. Where is its seat, and what branches exist at each [[Church Tiers|church tier]]?
44. How does it staff itself — ordinary Cinders, orphan cohorts, or hired lay accountants and merchants?
45. Where is the line between Shared Hearth and the enforcers: when it finds hoarding or embezzlement, does it act itself or hand the case to Silent Illumination, [[The Chancellery of the Purifying Flame|Purifying Flame]], or the courts?

## My takes on your questions

**Q4, interest.** The case for allowing it: without it there are no lending institutions, credit dries up, and commerce cannot fund the very works you want. A lender also takes real risk and gives real service, and the alternative, a ban, drives lending underground where rates are far worse. The case against: interest pays for time alone, not labour, which cuts against your own "value of labour" logic; the poor borrow from need and pay the most, so the rate is regressive; and compounding turns need into servitude. If you allow it, the doctrine needs a principle rather than a number. The cleanest is that interest may only cover real risk and real service, never scarcity or the borrower's desperation, with a cap enforced by the state rather than the Church. That keeps the Church's hands clean and gives the state a job.

**Q5, the vow.** Your answer rules out "poverty" and points at "not rich." Suggestions to accept or reject: **the Vow of Sufficiency**, **the Vow of the Steady Flame** (a flame that lights a room without burning down the house), or **the Vow of the Open Hand**. All three say enough without saying destitute.

**Q7, inheritance.** Your instinct is fair but the mechanism does not yet work. The value of a parent's labour is not observable. Much inherited wealth comes from land, luck, and connections, not labour, so a labour-value tax cannot be computed from the facts. It also invites evasion: gifts before death, trusts, moving assets beyond the border, and it leans on a strong state the Church cannot command, since it is apolitical. Better levers: a one-time levy on inherited wealth, a cap on how much may pass untaxed, or simply moral pressure to give the inheritance away, backed by the same hoarding test the living face. Any of those sidesteps the valuation problem.

**Q9, acceptable wealth.** You asked what I meant. I meant that your doctrine quietly needs a category of wealth it does not condemn, or the whole thing collapses into "any rich person is guilty." Your answer supplies it: wealth is acceptable by *use*, not by amount. Money that circulates toward others is fine; money kept back is not. The catch is that use is hard to see, which is why I suspect the pledge system is the machinery that makes "acceptable wealth" verifiable. That is why I keep pressing on question 17.

**Q12, solar harvesting.** It fits better than you think, because your canon already has the [[Holy Ground Project]] running on [[The Chancellery of Prismatic Inquiry|Prismatic Inquiry]] solar-prism batteries. Revenue options: enchanted goods and batteries sold as town infrastructure (everlight streets, heat, wards), alchemical goods from Prismatic Inquiry, healing from [[The Chancellery of Consecrated Solace|Consecrated Solace]], and courier service through the [[The Ember Post|Ember Post]]. The framing question is whether these count as the Church profiting, which your non-profit line forbids, or as mission-funded works whose surplus returns to relief. Kingmaker's settlement rules would let a Church-held structure generate income without breaking the fiction.

**Q13, land and rents.** Three options. Strict: the Church holds no productive land at all, churches sit on donated plots, and any land it receives is sold or given to the state, which fits your answer that the state owns production. Middle: the Church leases what it is given at capped rents that fund relief directly. Trust: the Church holds land in trust for a community and extracts nothing from it. The strict option fits your doctrine best, but it sharpens the funding problem you already spotted: with production in state hands and only contracts and donations left, the Church is fragile in any state that stops paying. That fragility is worth naming as a real weakness of the system, not hiding.
