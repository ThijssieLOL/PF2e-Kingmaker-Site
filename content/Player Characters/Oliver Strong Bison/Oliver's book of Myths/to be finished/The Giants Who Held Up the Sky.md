## The story
An ancient giant myth tells of a time when the sky itself began to fall toward the earth.

At first, the people below believed it was the end of the world. Mountains shook beneath the weight of the heavens, the seas rose, and the light of the sun grew dim. The gods gathered and tried to hold the sky in its place, but even their combined strength was not enough.

Then the giants came.

One by one, they gathered beneath the heavens and raised their hands. Some stood upon the highest mountains, while others planted their feet firmly in the earth. Each giant took hold of a piece of the falling sky, bearing its weight upon their shoulders.

They remained there for seven days and seven nights.

They did not sleep. They did not eat. They did not ask who would thank them when it was over. They simply held up the heavens, because someone had to.

At the end of the seventh night, the sky returned to its proper place. The mountains stopped trembling, the seas settled, and the light of the sun returned.

The gods, grateful for what the giants had done, offered them great treasures as payment. Gold, weapons, lands, and even places among the heavens were offered.

The giants refused them all.

*"We did not hold up the sky for treasure,"* they said. *"We held it because it was falling."*

Instead, they asked only one thing of the gods.

*"Remember who held the sky when it began to fall."*

The gods agreed, and from that day onward, the story was passed from generation to generation.

This is why many giants believe that strength is not a privilege to be enjoyed, but a responsibility to be carried. The stronger someone becomes, the greater the burdens they are expected to bear. A giant who uses their strength only for themselves is said to have forgotten the lesson of the first giants.

---

## Moral of the story

*Those strong enough to carry the burden should use their strength to help those who cannot.*
