> "The shepherd walks behind the flock. A crown weighs a neck; a rod only guides a hand."
> — Aurelius

## Overview

The Shepherd is Aurelius's notebook of political theory and game theory, bound in scuffed brown leather and carried in the book case he calls the Capsa. Its spine began as a scribe's copy of an old treatise on princes, a text that taught rulers to be feared rather than loved. Aurelius kept the copy, cut away the pages he could not answer, and wrote over the rest. Chapter by chapter he amends the old doctrine with his own: a prince seizes a realm, but a shepherd steers a flock he does not own. The book studies the living the way the [[Spirits Ledger]] studies the dead, and it rests in the bag beside [[The Hymnal]] so that the three keep one another honest.

### Quick Facts
- **Kind of Book:** Personal notebook of political theory and game theory
- **Model:** A working answer to an old treatise on princes
- **Purpose:** To test how power is steered without being seized
- **Kept In:** The leather book case Aurelius calls the Capsa
- **State:** Half written; most chapters wait for the Stolen Lands

## The Chapters

Each chapter carries a title and a few pages of notes. The rest of every page is blank, kept for the field to fill.

### On the Rod and the Flock
A shepherd's authority is borrowed. The flock lends it for the flock's own good, and the day a shepherd uses it against the flock it is gone. This is his first and firmest amendment to the old treatise: power is stewardship, never ownership.

### On the Fox and the Lion
From the old treatise he keeps the two beasts. A ruler needs the lion's force to be feared and the fox's cunning to be trusted. Where the old text reads both as tricks of the ruler, Aurelius notes that both are signals. What a ruler does on a visible day teaches the flock what to expect on an invisible one, and the flock watches more closely than any court.

### On Virtue and Fortuna
The old treatise tells a prince to trust his own virtue and bind fortune early. Aurelius glosses the margin: a shepherd can keep the flock ready but cannot promise the storm away. When the storm comes, preparation is what looks like luck.

### On the Wolf at the Door
Tyranny, whether it grows inside a kingdom or walks in from outside. He lists the sicknesses in his hand: rulers who hoard, councils who flatter, churches that crown themselves. On the last page of this chapter he copied the [[Trias Politicas]] in full, his answer to the wolf.

### On the Three Pillars
The longest chapter, written and rewritten. The [[Trias Politicas]] divides power into three pillars, the Crown for law, the Military for defense, the Church for moral judgment, each too weak to rule alone and strong enough to check the others. His own rule appears twice, underlined: clergy never sit on thrones.

## Exercises in the Margins

The back pages hold his games: bargains between prisoners, auctions of loyalty, signals sent in the dark. He plays them alone during long watches and by letter with his siblings, [[Vespera]] answering move for move. Every entry is dated and answered in a second hand of ink, so the book reads like a long argument carried across years.
