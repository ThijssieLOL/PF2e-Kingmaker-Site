> "Every spell is a verse, and every verse is a promise kept."
> — Aurelius

## Overview

Copy this block into [[The Hymnal]] under the spell's rank, then fill it in. The verse opens with its mechanics, written out in full: rank, traits, actions, range, targets, and the components it uses. The fields after that hold the flavor, what Aurelius sees, says, and feels when he casts. Keep his spoken words in the [[The Liturgical Cadence|Liturgical Cadence]]. Never invent mechanics; the rules come from the spell as written.

## The Verse
- **Name, Rank & Traits:** (e.g., *Stabilize*, cantrip 1, Necromancy, healing, vitality; two actions, somatic and verbal)

## The Sight of It
What the spell looks like as it leaves Aurelius: the shape of the Aureolin White flame, whether it is physical fire or soul-light, and what it does to the air and shadows around him. His tattoos ignite as he casts; note when the glow is full and when it barely wakes.

## Verbal Component
The words the spell is carried on: a line of scripture, a hymn verse, a spirit's name, or a single word held long. Written in the Liturgical Cadence, formal and rhythmic, with no modern contractions. This is the spell's verbal component, so the words must be spoken for the spell to take hold.

## Somatic Component
The motion that shapes the spell: which hand, how the fingers move, whether it is a seal drawn in the air, a hand laid flat, or a single finger raised like a pen. His fingers trace holy patterns and scribe's flourishes. This is the spell's somatic component.

## Material Component
What the spell consumes or needs in hand: a pinch of ash, a prayer bead, a breath of candle smoke, or nothing at all. Where the spell lists no material component, say so plainly.

## The Echo
What casting feels like inside him: the internal heat, where it gathers, and whether the verse comes easy or costs him. Note the strain of a strong spell and the recoil when his focus breaks.

## The Afterglow
What remains once the spell is done: a fading warmth, a scent of sun-baked stone, a soft ringing silence, or a trail of embers that cools to nothing.

## Notes
Where he recorded the verse, who taught it to him, or why he keeps it near the front of the book.
