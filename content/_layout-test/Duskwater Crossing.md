# Duskwater Crossing

> "A brief atmospheric description or quote."

## Overview

Duskwater Crossing is a river ford town of some three hundred people on the frontier of the Stolen Lands. It began as a ferry landing and grew into a market town once a parish of the Church of the Eternal Blazing Sun took root on the bank, and the river still sets the pace of its days.

### Quick Facts

- **Terrain Type:** River ford town
- **Region:** The Stolen Lands
- **Population:** About 300
- **Controlled By:** Town mayor and council
- **Status:** Active

---

## Description

The town stands where the river spreads wide and shallow over a gravel shelf, the only dependable crossing for a day's ride. Houses of timber and river stone line the north bank, their lower rooms raised on piles against the spring rise. A wooden palisade closes the landward side, and a volunteer militia drills in the field behind it.

### The Ford

The ford is the town's reason to exist. Traders bound for the frontier time their journeys to reach it by afternoon, and on market days the gravel shelf carries more traffic than the road. When the water runs high the ford closes and the ferry takes over.

### The Town

A single square holds the market and the town's meetings. The mill stands at the upstream end of the bank and the church at the downstream end, so the two face each other across the settled streets.

## History

### A Ferry and a Ford

For years the crossing was one boat and one ferryman's family. Goods bound upriver and downriver changed hands on the bank, and a few households stayed through the winter.

### The Parish

The [[The Church of the Eternal Blazing Sun|Church of the Eternal Blazing Sun]] built a Tier 1 parish on the bank, and the settlement grew up around it. The parish brought steady work and a school, along with rites that frontier families could not otherwise reach.

### The Toll Bridge

The ferry once gave way to a toll bridge, and for a time the crossing paid for itself. A raid years ago left the bridge half-burnt, and the town never rebuilt it. The piers still stand in the river and the blackened span hangs above the water, so travellers use the ford again.

---

## Economy

River trade keeps the town fed. Grain, wool, and timber go downriver; salt, tools, and finished goods come back up. A flat-bottomed ferry poles across when the ford runs too deep, and its fare is the oldest charge in the town.

### The Mill and the Market

The grain mill grinds for the farms on both banks and runs from first light to last. On market day the square fills with farmers, fishers, and the crews of the flatboats, and most of the week's business is settled before dusk.

---

## The Parish

The town's Tier 1 parish is run by a Grade 7 (Blaze) priest and two Grade 8 (Cinder) clergy. It is the smallest house the Church fields, a [[Church Tiers|tier]] meant for a village rather than a city, and the two Cinders share the rites, the ledgers, and the school between them. The parish keeps a message hearth for the [[The Ember Post|ember post]], and its cremation fire serves the whole district.

### Tithes and the Ford

The mayor and the parish disagree about money. The parish preaches the [[The Shared Hearth Economy|Shared Hearth]]: the tithe is a gift set by a household's ability, never a levy, and the Church counts it a wrong for a town to demand religious charity it will not pay for. The mayor answers that three hundred people cannot keep a ford alone. The ford is repaired late most years while the argument runs.

## Points of Interest

### The Old Toll Bridge

The half-burnt bridge stands on its stone piers mid-river, close enough to walk to in low water. Children dare each other onto the span and fishers cast from the piers. The burnt timber has rotted where it fell, and the town has left it there.

### The Parish Church

A small stone church with the sun cut into its gable, its doors open at dawn and dusk for the two Sunday services. The message hearth and the cremation fire both sit within its walls.

### The Ford

The gravel shelf itself, wide enough for a wagon in the dry season and marked by the stones that boatmen move when the bed shifts.

## Encounters & Hazards

### Spring Flooding

Every spring the river rises and the lower streets go under. Doorframes carry high-water marks, and households in the lowest houses keep a boat tied to the porch. The flood closes the ford for days and turns the square into a shallow lake.

### The Palisade and the Militia

The palisade has not been tested since the raid that burned the bridge. Every household owes a season of militia drill, and the town keeps a watch on the upstream road when strangers are about.

## Quests & Secrets

### The Drowned Chapel

Townsfolk say a chapel lies under the river, drowned when the bed shifted, and that its bell rings on still nights. Old fishers claim to know the spot and will not cross it after dark. The parish keeps no record of any such chapel and will say no more about it.
