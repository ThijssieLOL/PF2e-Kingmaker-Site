# The Vestments of the Clergy

> "Let your raiment be a litany, that the faithful may read at a glance your grade, your pillar, and your penance."
> — Maxim recited at the consecration of every vestment

## Overview

The clergy of [[The Church of the Eternal Blazing Sun]] is dressed by a twofold system, as ordered as the faith itself. Every cleric wears a **primary vestment** fixed by their grade in the fire hierarchy — the rope-bound cassock of the Spark, the embroidered stole of the Cinder, the cloth-of-gold of the Zenith — and over it, or beside it, a **secondary vestment** fixed by their chancellery, marking the pillar of the Church they serve. Black, white, and gold are the colors of the faith; each chancellery lends its own accent, so that a cleric walking a cathedral floor reads as a walking catechism: the grade in the cloth, the pillar in the badge, the penance in the marks the vestments are cut to reveal.

### Quick Facts
- **Primary Vestment:** Fixed by grade, per [[The Grade System]]
- **Secondary Vestment:** Fixed by chancellery
- **Palette:** Black, white, and gold, with chancellery accents
- **Sacred Marks:** The [[Tattoos of Penance]] are displayed by law in daily ministry
- **Consecration:** Vestments are consecrated at ordination and re-consecrated at every ascent of grade

---

## The Twofold Vesture

- **The Primary Vestment:** The cassock, stole, and regalia of a cleric's grade. It changes only when the cleric ascends; its ornament is strictly graded, and no cleric may wear the cloth of a higher flame.
- **The Secondary Vestment:** The garment, badge, and accent color of a cleric's chancellery. It is worn when the cleric acts for their pillar — at the hearth, the bench, the archive, the infirmary, the garrison, the mission field — and marks the nine-fold division of the Church's labor.
- **The Law of the Unstolen Flame:** No cleric may wear the vestment of a higher grade, nor the secondary of a chancellery not their own. To take another's cloth is to steal light — an offense tried by [[The Chancellery of the Purifying Flame|the Purifying Flame]] as a grave breach of order.

## Primary Vestments by Grade

The [[The Grade System|grades of the Church]] are flames, and the vestments follow the fire: from the smallest spark to the noonday sun, the cloth grows finer, heavier, and more wrought with gold.

### Grade 9 — Spark
The entry grade is dressed in the humblest cloth in the Church: a plain cassock of coarse black wool, unornamented, closed with a white cord cincture knotted thrice, with a bare white collar and rough black shoes. The Spark wears no stole. The cassock is worn only within the church's walls; abroad, the Spark dresses as laity with a small brass sun-badge at the breast. The garment is itself a lesson — the lowest flame is the humblest, and every ornament to come is earned.

### Grade 8 — Cinder
The standard clergy — scribes, sacristans, and field agents such as [[Aurelius]] — wear the Church's classic dress: an ankle-length black cassock with a crisp high collar closed by gold frog-closures; the first stole, a long white stole embroidered with gold solar patterns and golden floral filigree, reaching to the shins; a sturdy brown leather belt with a circular golden buckle; polished black boots and thin leather gloves. The Cinder is the first grade permitted to wear the vestments beyond the church grounds, in public ministry — the stole is the mark of the Church abroad in the world.

### Grade 7 — Blaze
The parish priest's vestment refines the Cinder's: the cassock is cut from finer wool and gains gold piping along the seams, and the leather belt gives way to a gold-fringed cincture. The stole is widened and embroidered along its full length. For services, the Blaze wears the **rochet**, a knee-length white over-garment with a gold-embroidered hem, and the **blaze-cap**, a soft black cap bound in gold braid and bearing a small sun-emblem. The Blaze is the first grade whose vestments stand at the head of their own church.

### Grade 6 — Ignis
The bishop's vestment is cut from a silk-wool blend with gold brocade at the shoulders and hem. At high rites the Ignis wears the **sun-mitre**, a tall golden mitre bearing a sunburst, with the **pectoral sun** — a golden sunburst medallion on a chain at the breast — and the episcopal ring set with a sunstone. On the greatest feasts, the stole is cloth-of-gold.

### Grade 5 — Pyre
The archbishop adds the **solar pallium**: a white woollen band edged in gold, worn about the shoulders with a sunburst embroidered at breast and back. On high feasts the cassock may be of black velvet with cloth-of-gold panels, and the mitre jeweled.

### Grade 4 — Vesta
The keepers of the eternal lamp hold the **vestal white**: on major feasts the cassock itself is white silk wrought in gold — the cloth-of-light — while daily office is kept in black velvet with gold-brocade shoulders. The Vesta holds the right to the **great mantle**, a long black cappa magna bearing a gold sunburst on its train, the length of the train marking the office.

### Grade 3 — Zenith
The legendary figures of the faith wear stole and cuffs of cloth-of-gold, a jeweled pectoral and rings, and white gloves in all seasons — hands set apart. Their **zenith-cloak** is of black velvet lined with white silk, bearing a great sunburst across the back. On the highest feasts the cassock itself is cloth-of-gold.

### Grade 2 — Solstice
The supreme leader of the faith wears the **radiant vestments**: a white cassock of cloth-of-silver, fully brocaded in gold, beneath the **Radiant Crown**, a golden sunburst headdress, and in hand the **Solar Staff**. The great golden mantle is embroidered with the emblems of all nine chancelleries in a ring — for the Solstice commands them all — and the vestments are said to hold a faint, living warmth, the nearness of the goddess who chose their wearer.

### Grade 1 — Star
The saints are no longer dressed by the Church. They are depicted in radiant white and gold, the colors of the Sunlit Fields; their relics are enshrined in white silk, and their icons wear the vestments of their highest mortal grade rendered in gold.

### Grade 0 — The Eternal Blazing Sun
The goddess wears no vesture but her own light.

## Secondary Vestments by Chancellery

Where the primary vestment is the same in every archdiocese, the secondary is the dress of the pillar: nine garments, nine accents, nine emblems. It is worn over or beside the grade cassock when a cleric acts for their chancellery, and its absence is as telling as its presence — in the field, in disguise, and in danger, the secondary is set aside with the primary.

### [[The Chancellery of Silent Illumination]]
- **Garment:** The **shadow-cloak** — a hooded cloak of soot-charcoal wool lined with pale silver-grey silk.
- **Accent:** Soot-grey and silver.
- **Emblem:** A single silver ray falling through darkness.
- **Wearing:** Plain by law. The cloak is deliberately unadorned, cut to swallow the shape and hush the step; the silver lining flashes only when the cloak turns — the light that moves in silence. Members in public cover — the scribe [[Vespera]], the cantor [[Julian]], the sacristan [[Lyra]] — wear it unmarked and folded, or leave it in the vestry altogether: for the silent arm, concealment is itself the vestment.

### [[The Chancellery of the Shared Hearth]]
- **Garment:** The **hearth-apron** — a rough-spun amber apron, sturdy and washable, worn with an alms-purse on a long strap.
- **Accent:** Hearth-amber.
- **Emblem:** An open hearth with a rising flame.
- **Wearing:** The apron of service, worn at the soup kitchen, the orphanage, and the treasury counter. It is the most handled vestment in the Church and is meant to be soiled in the work of charity; the Church teaches that an apron stained with honest work is holier than an unstained stole.

### [[The Chancellery of Prismatic Inquiry]]
- **Garment:** The **prism-mantle** — a light, practical over-mantle of silver-white with a hem threaded in the seven colors.
- **Accent:** Silver-white and the spectrum.
- **Emblem:** A prism splitting a single ray into seven colors.
- **Wearing:** Worn in laboratories, observatories, and alchemical works, its pockets cut for instruments and specimens; the rainbow hem mirrors the doctrine that the goddess's light, examined truly, holds every truth within its spectrum.

### [[The Chancellery of Sacred Canon]]
- **Garment:** The **scholar's hood** — an ink-blue academic hood worn over the shoulders, with a breviary satchel on a long strap.
- **Accent:** Ink-blue.
- **Emblem:** An open book beneath a ray of light.
- **Wearing:** The hood of the teacher, scribe, and examiner, worn in the schoolroom and at the Theological Examinations; its ink-blue is the color of scripture ink and of the night sky in which the stars — the saints — burn.

### [[The Chancellery of the Purifying Flame]]
- **Garment:** The **judicial mantle** — a flame-russet half-cloak worn over one shoulder, fastened with a scale-shaped clasp.
- **Accent:** Flame-russet.
- **Emblem:** A balance above a flame.
- **Wearing:** Worn at trials, inquisitions, and the pronouncement of the Moral Veto. The russet is the color of the forge-fire that burns away dross, and the scale-shaped clasp is a reminder that the judge's own deeds are weighed in the same balance.

### [[The Chancellery of the Outer Horizon]]
- **Garment:** The **horizon-capelet** — the white traveller's poncho-capelet of the road, bearing the horizon emblem at the breast and lined with dawn-blue.
- **Accent:** Dawn-blue.
- **Emblem:** The sun half-risen over a straight horizon.
- **Wearing:** The vestment of the mission field. Every cleric on the road wears the white traveller's capelet, but the Outer Horizon's own is the same garment marked with the half-risen sun — so a missionary abroad carries their pillar with them across every border. [[Aurelius]] wears his capelet with the same gold solar trim as his formal stole.

### [[The Chancellery of Celestial Aegis]]
- **Garment:** The **aegis-surcoat** — a sun-white surcoat worn over armor or cassock, emblazoned with a golden shield-sunburst.
- **Accent:** Sun-white and silvered steel.
- **Emblem:** A sunburst behind a shield.
- **Wearing:** The vestment of the garrison and the war-fleet, and the only secondary cut for battle: it is worn by temple guardians and paladin orders in the field, and by their chaplains at the camp altar.

### [[The Chancellery of Consecrated Solace]]
- **Garment:** The **solace-tabard** — a sleeveless tabard of pale ash-white hemmed in gold, worn with sleeves rolled and pinned.
- **Accent:** Ash-white and warm cream.
- **Emblem:** A flame cradled in cupped hands.
- **Wearing:** The vestment of the infirmary and the funeral pyre, cut short and sleeveless for work at the bedside and the crematorium; its gold hem is the promise that the flame which consumes the body holds the soul gently.

### [[The Chancellery of the Meridian Cadastre]]
- **Garment:** The **ledger-sash** — a broad archive-grey sash crossed over the breast, fitted with small pockets for quills, seals, and register keys.
- **Accent:** Archive-grey with ember-red.
- **Emblem:** An open register crossed by a quill.
- **Wearing:** The vestment of the archive, the registry, and the census; its pockets are counted, and its ember-red thread marks the hearth-keepers of the [[The Ember Post|Ember Post]], who wear also the brass **ember-clasp** at the collar.

## The Law of the Vestments

- **When the vestments are worn:** The primary vestment is worn whenever a cleric acts in the name of the Church — at service, court, audience, and rite. The secondary is worn for chancellery duty: at the treasury, the bench, the archive, the infirmary, the garrison, the mission field. At solemn synods and great feasts, both are worn together in full.
- **When they are set aside:** In covert work, dangerous mission fields, and disguise, the vestments are set aside for plain dress without shame — the Church values the mission over the garment. A traveller's hood pulled low, as [[Aurelius]] wears it, is itself a recognized vestment of the road.
- **The [[Tattoos of Penance]]:** Daily vestments are cut to display the marks of humility — cassock sleeves that roll and pin, hems above the shin — so that the brands remain on view in ordinary ministry. High ceremony covers them with formal gloves; covert work covers them with long sleeves. The law of the faith holds that the vestments must never be used to hide what the goddess has chosen to reveal.
- **The Law of the Unstolen Flame:** No cleric may wear the vestment of a higher grade, nor the secondary of a chancellery not their own; to take another's cloth is to steal light. The offense is tried by [[The Chancellery of the Purifying Flame]] and is treated as a breach of order as grave as the sin it conceals.
- **The Rite of the Rising Flame:** When a cleric ascends a grade, their old vestments are consigned to the holy fire in a private rite before the new are donned — flame yields to flame, as the body is yielded at the pyre. The new vestments are worn for the first time before the congregation at the next public service.
