## Overview

The Shared Hearth is the economic doctrine of [[The Church of the Eternal Blazing Sun]]: the teaching that governs how the faithful earn, hold, and give wealth. [[The Chancellery of the Shared Hearth]] administers it.

### Quick Facts
- **Owner:** [[The Church of the Eternal Blazing Sun]]
- **Steward:** [[The Chancellery of the Shared Hearth]]
- **Core Tenet:** Giving is a civic duty, and wealth is for use, not hoarding.
- **Status:** Active

---

## Wealth & Giving

- **Means of Production:** The Church does not own the means of production directly. It urges the state to hold most of them, and private owners may hold the rest. Corruption is condemned wherever it appears.
- **No Ceiling on Income:** A high income is not a sin in itself. The doctrine judges what a person does with wealth, not how much they earn. A person who lives modestly and spends the rest on others commits no sin, however much they earn.
- **The Common Creed:** The Church teaches that each gives according to their ability and receives according to their need.
- **Civic Duty:** Giving is a civic duty as much as a religious one.
- **Progressive Tithe:** Tithes are expected of all believers. The rate is not fixed; it rises with income.
- **Spendable Income:** The tithe is assessed on spendable income, what remains after a household's basic necessities. What counts as a basic necessity is not yet defined.
- **Judged by Ability:** A person who can barely support their family may owe nothing.

### The Clergy's Vow
Clergy do not vow poverty. They vow not to lead rich lives.

---

## Funding & Banking

- **Non-Profit:** The Church earns no profit of its own.
- **State Contracts:** It draws steady income from state contracts, chiefly for organizing and managing public charities such as orphanages and soup kitchens.
- **Donations:** The rest comes from donations. Where a state will not pay for its charities, the Church funds them itself and asks donors for fixed pledged amounts.
- **A Moral Wrong:** The Church holds it a moral wrong for a state to demand its charitable works without paying for them, or to reject those works entirely.
- **Banking:** The Church does not run banks. Banking is left to the state, or to private owners where no foul play such as monopoly is present. The Church prefers the state.
