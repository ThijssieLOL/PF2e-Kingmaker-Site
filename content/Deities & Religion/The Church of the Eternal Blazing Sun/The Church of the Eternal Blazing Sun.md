## Overview

The Church of the Eternal Blazing Sun is a large, Lawful Good religious institution that worships the sun goddess. Its clergy value redemption, forgiveness, and the preservation of life above all else. The faith is highly organized, with a grade-based hierarchy, a firm rule against involvement in state governance, and an economic philosophy built around caring for the community.

The Church does not seek to rule nations. It sees itself as a moral guardian of the mortal realms, and it intervenes, by force if necessary, when a secular ruler becomes corrupt or commits grave evils.

### Quick Facts
- **Deity:** [[Erisol]]
- **Alignment:** Lawful Good
- **Leader:** Grade 2 (Solstice), chosen directly by the sun goddess
- **Structure:** Grade-based hierarchy (Grade 9 to Grade 0) and nine High Chancelleries
- **Status:** Active

## Theology & Cosmology

### [[Erisol|The Sun Goddess]]
The Church worships the sun goddess Erisol, known as the Eternal Blazing Sun or simply the Sun. Her believers are called **solites**. The Church acknowledges that other true gods exist, but it teaches that the Sun is the most morally good of them and therefore the only one worthy of mortal worship.

### [[The Star Saints]]
The goddess raises ascended mortals to sainthood, and each saint is represented by a sacred star or constellation. Seventeen are recorded, with their constellations and domains.

### Afterlife & Purification
Solites believe in a heaven-like afterlife, informally called the Sunlit Fields. When a solite dies, the soul passes through something like purgatory, where holy fire gently burns away any remaining sins. The soul then rests eternally in the goddess's light.

The Church has no official position on where non-believers go after death. It says only that they do not enter the Sunlit Fields.

### Cremation & The Undead
The Church teaches that the undead are entirely soulless, and it opposes them fiercely. Creating an undead creature, or allowing one to be created, insults the dead: it traps the body in a mocking half-existence and keeps the soul from resting.

For this reason, bodies must be cremated with holy fire. Cremation keeps the corpse from being desecrated or raised as an undead thrall, and it secures the soul's passage to the afterlife. Fire is the Church's primary element, used for purification and as a weapon against darkness.

## Hierarchy & Formation

Rank in the Church is earned rather than granted, and the ladder runs from the schoolroom to the highest grade.

### [[The Grade System]]
The hierarchy is a ladder of fire-themed grades. It begins at Grade 9 (Spark) and ends at Grade 2 (Solstice), the head of the Church. Grade 1 (Star) and Grade 0 are reserved for saints and the goddess herself. Each title names a larger flame than the last. Higher grades bring more authority, access to restricted archives, higher pay, and an easier claim on powerful relics. From Cinder upward, a member must serve in a growing number of Chancelleries.

- **[[The Ascension Trials]]:** Each step up requires passing the yearly examinations in Combat, Specialization, and Doctrine. The Review Board, a council of clergy one grade higher, judges the candidate.

### [[The Church Schooling System]]
The Church runs the whole path from first lessons to the top of its orders.

- **[[The Church Schooling Program]]:** Church schools take a child from first lessons to the high school diploma. Any family may place a child, and a convicted orphan may take a place in a chancellery instead of serving a sentence.
- **[[The Chancellery Curriculum]]:** Above the diploma, the specialized track and the degrees belong to the chancellery. The Curriculum ties each degree to a step up the grades.

## Structure & Governance

### Regional Structure
- **Archdioceses:** The Church uses an archdiocese system adapted to the world's geography. Regional bishops and archbishops manage local parishes and stay in direct contact with the central headquarters.
- **Tactical Placement:** Churches are placed to reach as many people as possible for the resources spent, and higher-tier churches especially so. A church's tier therefore matches the population it serves.

### [[Church Tiers]]
Houses are tiered by the population they serve, from a Tier 1 parish run by a handful of Cinders up to the single Tier 5 headquarters. From Tier 2 upward, every church keeps the nine-fold staff. Higher tiers go to increasingly senior clergy, and each upgrade must be approved from above.

### The Chancelleries
The Church is administered much like a state. Rather than rely on informal orders, it divides executive authority among nine ministries, the High Chancelleries. Each is led by high-ranking clergy and governs one area of statecraft, divine law, or civil service. Each also sets an academic standard for the clergy who serve it, collected in [[The Chancellery Curriculum]].

### [[The Ember Post]]
The Church links its distant parishes and its field agents through a form of divine correspondence older than any courier road. A letter written in the right ink and burned turns into a swirl of embers, crosses any distance, and re-forms out of the fire of the person it was meant for.

## Political & Economic Philosophy

### The Radiant Mandate & Apolitical Stance
- **Separation of Power:** Clergy may not hold crowns, rule secular nations, or take direct political office.
- **The Moral Veto:** Although apolitical, the Church acts as a check on secular rulers. When a state becomes deeply corrupt or commits grave evils, [[The Chancellery of the Purifying Flame]] has the authority to intervene, with options ranging from public denouncement to a covert commando strike.

### [[The Shared Hearth Economy|Economic Stance]]
The Church views poverty as a failure of society rather than of the individual, and it holds the state morally responsible for its citizens' welfare. It runs one of the largest charitable operations in the world.

High income is no sin, but hoarding is. The strong and wealthy have a divine obligation to support the weak.

## Worship & Daily Life

### Liturgical Services
Mass is held daily. Sunday is a rest day with two main services, one at dawn and one at dusk.

### Gestures & Greetings
The common greeting is to draw a large counterclockwise circle over the chest with the right thumb, index, and middle fingers pinched together, while saying *"Praise the sun."* This gesture is called the Solar Circle.

Common responses:
- *"Praise the lady."* (quick, informal)
- *"Her light will overcome the darkness."* (hopeful)
- *"May her radiance guide your path."* (formal, typically used by clergy)
- *"And the darkness shall never claim you."* (reassuring)
- *"Shine bright, brother/sister."* (friendly, casual)
- *"From dawn to dusk, she watches."* (solemn)

### [[The Liturgical Cadence]]
Clergy speak in the Liturgical Cadence, a formal register with no modern contractions and plain, active verbs.

### [[The Vestments of the Clergy|Clergy Attire]]
Clergy dress by grade for ceremony and by chancellery for duty, and they wear their chancellery's emblem on the ceremonial vestment. Ceremonial vestments are an ankle-length black cassock with a high collar and gold engraving. A Grade 7 Blaze adds a long white stole worked in gold floral and solar patterns. In the field, clergy wear a dark tunic and trousers under a white traveler's poncho-capelet. The vestments include no armor, so a cleric who expects combat wears armor as a separate kit.

### [[Tattoos of Penance]]
The Church regards its clergy as redeemed sinners rather than flawless figures. Newly initiated clergy receive the [[Tattoos of Penance]], a permanent record of their past transgressions, the goddess's mercy, and their lifelong vow of public humility.

## Relations with Other Faiths

### Other Orthodox Churches
The Church keeps diplomatic relations with the faiths of other mainstream, benevolent deities, a circle it calls the Concordance of True Faiths. Because it holds the sun goddess to be the pinnacle of moral goodness, its dealings with them often carry a gentle condescension.

It counts [[The Church of the Everlasting Moonlight]] and [[The Order of the Sacred Mother]] among the well-aligned orthodox faiths, keeps good relations with both, and cooperates with them from time to time.

### [[The Grand Order of the Phoenix]]
A smaller denomination that split from the Church on good terms and remains in its good graces. It venerates the same sun goddess and departs from orthodoxy in practice rather than doctrine. Its most distinctive departure is the blood-blessed tradition of thrall-binding, a holy necromancy that never touches the dead.

### Other Religions
The Church keeps a file on the faiths it has not yet met, and nothing more. None is active in the lands where the Church is, so no relations have been opened.

- **[[The Celestial Calling]]:** A Neutral Good faith of spirits and callings, led by the greater spirit called the Sovereign Muse. It is not established where the Church is, so it exists for the Church only as a file and a name.

### Cults & Heresies
- **Cults of False Gods:** Cults of demon lords, evil deities, or entities that practice sacrifice and necromancy are treated as absolute threats. The Church sends its intelligence and military arms to dismantle them.
- **[[The Heralds of the Moon Incarnate]]:** A Groetus cult that works to hasten the end of the world, with demonic forces and ritual where preaching will not serve. It has not reached the lands where the Church is, so the Church holds a file on it and has not moved.
- **[[Followers of the Gray Sign]]:** A Groetus sect that preaches the end of the world without hastening it, and turns violent only rarely. The Church keeps a file on it and has had no dealings with it.
- **Heretics of the sun church:** The Church faces no rival sun-worshipping sect. Its heresies come from groups that claim to belong to the Church itself. These false brethren pose as orthodox clergy or faithful communities and preach doctrines the Church condemns, deceiving the laity from within. The Church treats them as the gravest form of heresy and hunts them through its inquisitorial and intelligence arms. They differ from [[The Grand Order of the Phoenix]], which split openly and remains in good standing. The false brethren are impostors, and the Church shows them none of the charity it gives to kindred denominations.

### Magic & Secularists
- **Atheists & Agnostics:** The Church sees them as misled, ignorant, or simply never exposed to the goddess's light, and it prioritizes missionary work to educate them.
- **Arcane & Secular Magic:** The Church fully accepts arcane spellcasters, alchemists, and others who do not draw their power from divine sources, provided they use their talents for good.

## History & Lore

### Origin Myth
The sun goddess and the other true gods shaped the cosmos, giving rise to the universe and the mortal world. The Church was founded in antiquity by the first Grade 2 (Solstice) of the faith, whose identity is lost to time.

### Historical Conflicts
The Church has fought in several major wars and holy crusades, particularly against rising undead threats and corrupt empires. The specific wars are not yet decided (see House Notes).

## Current Threats & Prophecies

### Internal Threats
The Church watches its own ranks closely. Clergy of Grade 3 or 4 may secretly hoard wealth or abuse their influence. Lower-grade priests may ignore local injustice to keep the peace with secular authorities. Younger clergy may try to soften the strict anti-undead doctrine or push for traditional burial.

### External Threats
The Church's greatest external struggle is with kingdoms that have banned the faith. These regimes see its economic teachings and anti-poverty advocacy as a direct threat to the ruling elite. The Church relies on covert missionary work to get around the bans, such as [[Aurelius]]'s mission in the Stolen Lands.

### Apocalyptic Prophecy
An ancient prophecy in the Church's secret archives foretells a great cosmic trial. Its content is not yet decided (see House Notes).
