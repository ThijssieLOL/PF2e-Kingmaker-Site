# Apparition Design Brainstorm

> Working document. This is not canon. It is where the spirit designs get argued out before anything lands on a family page or in a [[Spirit Template]] note. Every design below is a draft until it is confirmed.

## Purpose

Work out the look of Aurelius's apparition spirits, one per rank per family, before touching their personalities. The [[Spirits Ledger]] holds the system; this note holds the design rules and the drafts.

## Scope

- Every family gets a **different spirit at every rank**, one signature spirit per rank. Fourteen families, nine ranks each.
- As Aurelius reaches a new rank, he finds the new spirit and **releases the old one with honor**. The family ladder is a progression, not a collection. For example, the rank 1 spirit is released when he can carry the rank 2 spirit.
- Each signature spirit is tied to a **specific place in the Stolen Lands** where he finds it.
- The spirit world is **completely separate** from the physical one. Only spirit-seers (the [[Ocular of Kaelen]], spirit-seeing magic, an animist's attunement) can see spirits. No one else can see, smell, hear, or feel them. Designs are therefore private to Aurelius and the player.

## Confirmed design decisions

- **Rank correlates with intelligence and might, but it is not a personhood ladder.** A Level 9 spirit can be a mindless catastrophe. A Level 2 spirit can be sly and articulate. The correlation is a tendency, not a law.
- **Size is independent of rank.** A rank 1 spirit can be enormous, a rank 9 spirit can be small.
- **All three design sources are in play.** A spirit can be a fear made flesh, a monument that remembers, or raw elemental grandeur, and often two of those at once.
- **The register is cursed-spirit horror, played eerie.** The designs are grotesque and confrontational, in the vein of JJK curses: wrong bodies, misplaced human features, exposed anatomy. They should be scary, cool, and visibly powerful. But the fright is often calm rather than aggressive. The model is the stag: it does not menace, it grazes, and that is worse. Subtle environmental horror is not the target. A spirit that hides in the scenery is a trap, not a curse.
- **Humanoid and abstract forms both appear at every tier.** No fixed climb from person to phenomenon.
- **Every family ladder gets its own rhythm.** The mix of forms differs from family to family. No two families should read the same way.
- **No mortal-visible effects.** No auras, smells, or local phenomena that non-seers can perceive.

## Design principles

### The spirit is a creature first

Design the creature, not the spell. Start with what the spirit is and what it does all day: what it eats, where it nests, what it herds or hoards or tends, how it moves, what it wants. Give it a life that would make sense even if it never met Aurelius. The apparition spell is the creature's capability, the thing that falls out of what it is. A fire spirit does not have to look like fire. The stag carries Monstrosity Form at Level 8, and it is not a picture of that spell; it is a grazing animal whose nature happens to be monstrous. A design that answers "what does the spell do" stops too early. A design that answers "what is this creature" already has the spell inside it.

### The origin sets the body plan

What the spirit was born from picks its shape. A single family produces every kind of spirit, because its domain has killed people, beasts, places, crowds, and ideas. This is what lets a humanoid and a monstrosity share a family without either feeling out of place.

| Born from | Body plan | Reads as |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| A person | Person | A human silhouette with one detail wrong |
| A beast | Beast | An exaggerated animal |
| A place | Colossus | A landscape; you only ever see a part of it |
| An event | Event | Weather or a phenomenon with intent |
| A crowd | Swarm | Many bodies acting as one |
| An idea or a fear | Object or absence | A thing that should not be alive, or a shape defined by what is missing |

### The wrongness is in the body

A cursed spirit is a creature you see, and what you see is a body that should not exist. The violation is physical and immediate, never hidden in the scenery. Human features sit where they do not belong: an eye in a stone, a mouth across a chest, a hand where a foot should be, a face stretched over something that was never meant to have one. The recognition is instant. You do not discover the wrongness; you look, and your body already knows. This is the JJK look: grotesque, confrontational, and impossible to mistake for a natural creature.

### The one wrong thing

The body carries **one signature violation.** One, not five. It is the single detail the eye lands on and cannot leave. The rest of the body should read as a real creature, so the violation has something normal to break. The stag is a stag before it is anything else; the ribcage of eyes is what ruins it. Tie the violation to the origin so it feels inevitable rather than random. One violation makes a curse; five make noise.

### The horror is in the behavior

A spirit is not a threat display. The stag does not hunt; it grazes, and you are in the pasture. The designs that work do something ordinary with terrible consequences, and they are calm while doing it. Indifference frightens more than aggression, because aggression can be answered and indifference cannot. Look for the moment the eye lands on the creature and the mind supplies the logic on its own: it is doing something, and you are part of what it is doing.

### The Avatar is the family's final shape

Every family page lists an **Avatar** in its Mechanical Reference. That is the family's ultimate silhouette, and the nine spirits can be read as the ladder leading toward it. It also tells you whether a family's top ranks should lean humanoid (Monarch of the Fey Courts: *Queen of the Winter Court*; Speaker in Sibilance: *Medusa of Merciless Mysteries*) or monstrous (Lurker in Devouring Dark: *Tentacles from the Dark*; Stalker in Darkened Boughs: *Beast of the Boughs*).

## Why the drafts come out boring

The principles above are correct, and every one of them is a filter. Not scenery. Not five violations. Not a spell with fur on it. Nothing in this note says where a design comes from. A model given a filter and no source samples the middle of everything it has read, which is a plain animal or a plain person with one detail wrong. That is what the two ladders are full of. The stag and the whale are not the products of the note; they are the exceptions that happened to fall at the edge of the distribution.

Second, the eighteen drafts all wear the same formula: a natural creature, plus one misplaced body part, plus calm behaviour. The formula is right. Worn eighteen times, it stops being a formula and becomes wallpaper.

Third, the liked designs are not liked for the reason this note assumes. The stag, the whale and the blackthorn have nothing to do with eye clusters or with mouths. All three have **broken their own shape**. The stag is an animal carrying a landscape. The whale is an animal at landscape scale. The blackthorn is a thing that keeps changing body plan. Everything that failed is still the right size and shape for what it is: a wolf, a hunter on all fours, a fat beast, a knot of fused hunters. None of those could not have grown. That is the real test, and it is sharper than "one wrong thing".

Fourth, "the one wrong thing" is being read as "one wrong organ". A misplaced eye is the weakest violation available, because it is a normal animal with a sticker on it. The strong violations are wrong **material**, wrong **scale**, wrong **quantity**, and **absence**. Placement is the only one the drafts ever use.

Fifth, the rule says to read the apparition spell last. The tables read left to right and put the spell second, so the spell becomes the brief. A spirit whose brief is *Fireball* is a Fireball with fur on it.

And the structural cause, which is worth naming plainly. `AGENTS.md` §7.1 forbids the writing agent from inventing: a braindump gets a questionnaire, never a draft, and unanswered stays unanswered. §6 then runs every note through the `humanizer` skill, whose whole job is to strip excess, inflated imagery and baroque phrasing. So the one agent with access to this vault is instructed not to invent, and then to sand the invention flat. It cannot do this job as written. Spirit designs need a place to be invented freely, and this note is that place. They must not be humanized after the fact: the humanizer removes exactly the excess this genre runs on.

## The generator

**1. The spirit must break its own shape.** A design is a cursed spirit only if the thing could not have grown. At least one rupture must be present.

| Rupture | What it is | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Scale** | A body at the wrong size for its own plan | The stag carrying a grove |
| **Plan** | A body that keeps changing, belongs to two kinds of thing, or is defined by what it is missing | The blackthorn wearing every shape |
| **Material** | A body of a substance that is not flesh, in a quantity that should not exist, held all the way through | The stack of hands |

Anything that passes none of the three is a monster, not a curse. Kill it before it reaches a table.

**2. Commit to one material, and let the material choose the palette.** Colour is a consequence of substance, not decoration. Ask what the body is made of and what that material does when light hits it. The Rainbow Dragon reads the way it does because its colour has a rule: iridescent scale, so the colour shifts with the angle. A palette without a rule is a mood board.

**3. The origin is a thesis about people, not an anecdote.** "A hunter killed and skinned by his own quarry" is a story, and stories earn sympathy. A thesis earns fear. Kaelerum has its own supply: a land every neighbour claims was stolen from them, charters handed to strangers, fey debts, bandits, cyclops ruins, and a census and a tithe arriving before the fields are ploughed.

**4. The face carries a feeling.** The violation needs an expression: pleased, shy, apologetic, bored, proud, sleepy, rapt. The stag works because it is unconcerned. Menace is the register the drafts keep reaching for, and menace is the least frightening one available.

**5. It has a job, and the spell is a side effect.** Design the life first. The apparition spell is what that life can do when it is asked.

### The rhythm mechanism

This note asks for a different rhythm in every family. Here is the mechanism: assign a rupture type to each rank before designing anything, so the ladder varies along an axis the eye can feel.

| Family | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 |
| :--- | :- | :- | :- | :- | :- | :- | :- | :- | :- |
| Stalker | plan | material | plan | material | plan | material | quantity | scale | scale |
| Lurker | plan | material | quantity | plan | scale | plan | plan | plan | material |
| Steward | material | plan | material | plan | material | quantity | plan | scale | scale |
| Crafter | plan | plan | absence | material | material | material | plan | material | absence |
| Lamentation | plan | material | plan | material | material | absence | absence | quantity | scale |
| Echo | plan | absence | plan | quantity | scale | material | material | plan | material |
| Monarch | plan | material | absence | plan | quantity | material | plan | scale | scale |
| Witness | material | plan | quantity | material | plan | scale | plan | absence | scale |
| Vanguard | scale | plan | material | scale | plan | quantity | material | scale | plan |

Three ranks in a row sharing a rupture is a mistake. Vary it, or the ladder flattens.

### The banks

Materials worth committing to, pick one and hold it end to end: tallow, hair, teeth, oiled scale, dried glue, beeswax, rust, chalk, wet plaster, salt, blackthorn, iron filings, soap, smoked glass, tar, moss, cord, lacquer, brine, whalebone, horn, split leather, hoarfrost, thread, gravel, gauze, quicklime, red lead.

Theses for Kaelerum, drawn from the Stolen Lands rather than from generic fantasy: the certainty that this land belongs to someone else and that the someone is you. What a charter feels like to the people who were already here. The boredom of a siege that never comes. The greed that arrives with a map. The belief that clearing it and ploughing it makes it yours. A debt that outlives the debtor. A favour taken back with interest. Being the last of your kind left alive. What a tree thinks of the fence it was cut for. Being asked to say thank you. Being counted.

- **Origin:** The first person to dam a river, and the dam that learned to want.
- **The wrong thing:** She is not made of water. She is the holding, and when she moves, the rivers move, and the shape of the land goes with them.
- **Material and palette:** Clear water, mossy stone, one gold ring on a finger of water that never closes.
- **Face:** Composed. She is doing arithmetic.
- **Where found:** The confluence of the Sellen and the Little Sellen.

### Level 6 — Personal Ocean *(rupture: quantity)*
- **The creature and its life:** A sea inside the outline of a person, walking on dry land, full to the brim. It is one body holding a whole ocean and the ocean wants out. It wanders the coast, and when it stops moving the water gets higher.
- **Origin:** A sailor who swallowed the sea and did not drown.
- **The wrong thing:** There is far too much water for the shape that holds it. The body is a skin around a tide, and it sloshes when the body turns.
- **Material and palette:** Deep blue, whitecap at the collar, the pale of a drowned face inside the clear part.
- **Face:** Calm, and behind the calm a great deal of pressure.
- **Where found:** A beach below the Tuskwater.

### Level 7 — Dancing Fountain *(rupture: material)*
- **The creature and its life:** A fountain in a drowned city, still playing, rising and falling to a rhythm it has kept for centuries. It is fed by no spring. It is fed by everyone who ever drank from it, and they are still in the water.
- **Origin:** A city that went under a reservoir with its festival still in progress.
- **The wrong thing:** The fountain is made of its audience. The spray holds their shapes, the drops are their voices, and it has never stopped playing.
- **Material and palette:** Pale stone, a rainbow in the mist, and a dark green under the water.
- **Face:** Many, rising and falling in the water, all mid-song.
- **Where found:** Beneath the surface of Candlemere.

### Level 8 — Whirlpool *(rupture: scale)*
- **The creature and its life:** A hole in the river that goes down further than the river is deep. Everything near it goes in and does not come out, and it has been emptying the shallows since the first flood.
- **Origin:** The first thing the water ever swallowed, still hungry.
- **The wrong thing:** It is bigger underneath than it is on top. Look into it at the right angle and you can see the far side of the world through the water.
- **Material and palette:** Black water, a white spiral, and a light coming up from below that is not the sun.
- **Face:** None. It is a mouth, and it is patient.
- **Where found:** A bend of the Sellen where no boat will go.

### Level 9 — Wrathful Storm *(rupture: plan)*
- **The creature and its life:** The whole river, from source to sea, lifted into the air as one body, looking for somewhere to put itself down. It has no bed, so it is always falling, and the land beneath it is already gone. It is the flood that ended the age of the cyclops.
- **Origin:** A river that rose up and never came back down.
- **The wrong thing:** It is a river and a storm at once, and it is still a river: it has a current, a source, and a mouth, and the mouth is open over the Stolen Lands.
- **Material and palette:** Storm grey, lightning white, and the green of the land it is about to erase.
- **Face:** None that can be looked at. It is a horizon.
- **Where found:** Over the Sellen, in the last week before the flood.

## Open questions

1. **Humanoid vs monstrosity.** Per family, per rank, or per spirit from its origin? There is no rule yet.
2. **The Avatar.** Does the Avatar get a spirit of its own at Level 9, or does it stay a separate final form that no individual spirit reaches?
3. **The Steward at Level 9.** Is there room for a Steward that speaks, or does the family stay wordless?
4. **Output format.** One-line designs under each family page's Level section, or a full [[Spirit Template]] note per spirit, or one-liners on the family pages with full notes only for the spirits he actually binds?
5. **Released spirits.** When a lower-rank spirit is released, does it stay an ally in the spirit world and become a recurring presence, or fade out of the story?
6. **Names.** Do individual spirits get personal names, or are they known only by their design (the stag, the blackthorn)?
7. **Objectivity.** How much of what Aurelius sees is objective truth versus his own interpretation? He is the only witness.

## Design checklist

1. Pick the family and rank. Read the apparition spell last, not first.
2. Design the creature: what is it, what does it do all day, where does it live, how does it move?
3. Decide the origin: what was this spirit born from? Let the origin pick the body plan.
4. Add one signature violation. Keep the rest of the body a coherent creature.
5. Check that the horror is in the creature's behavior and logic, not only in its shape.
6. Let the apparition spell be the capability that follows from what the creature is.
7. Check that the wrongness is written on the body, not hidden in the scenery.
8. Describe the silhouette in a single sentence. If it takes two, it is two designs.
9. Tie it to a place in the Stolen Lands where Aurelius finds it.
10. Run the kill test: could this thing have grown? If yes, start again.
11. Name it, or decide it stays unnamed.
